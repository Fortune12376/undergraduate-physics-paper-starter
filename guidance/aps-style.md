# APS style for undergraduate physics papers

Use the active assignment requirements first and APS style where compatible. PRL-specific rules apply when required by the assignment or when preparing an actual PRL submission. Page numbers below are the printed pages of the [APS Journals Style Guide for Authors, November 2026](APS_Journals_Style_Guide_Authors022326_guzwhx.pdf). **APS rule** identifies an explicit requirement, prohibition or prescribed convention. **APS recommendation** identifies advice, a preference or a usual practice. **Warwick** identifies assessment criteria or supervisor guidance.

## Scientific argument and manuscript structure

- **APS recommendation.** Make the title concise and self-contained. Avoid unfamiliar abbreviations and unnecessary opening words. In a single self-contained abstract paragraph, summarize the subject, results and conclusions. APS asks experimental papers to identify what was studied, what was measured and under what conditions. For a simulation, identify the modelled system, observables and simulation conditions. (§II.A, p. 5; §II.E, p. 10.)
- **APS rule.** PRL uses title case and limits the abstract to 600 characters. The abstract cannot contain displayed equations, tables or numbered citations. If a source is essential there, give its citation details directly in the abstract. (§II.A, p. 5; §II.E, pp. 10–11.)
- **APS recommendation and rule.** Freestanding headings are uncommon in PRL. When using PRL-style main headings, use an indented italic run-in heading with initial capitalization and an em dash before the paragraph text. Present figures, tables, equations and references in a logical sequence. (§II.G, p. 11; §II.G.1.c, p. 14.)

## Concise prose, equations and notation

- **APS recommendation.** Limit acronyms and use each for the same concept throughout. Spell out a one-off acronym in the abstract. Define recurring abbreviations at first use in the abstract and again at first use in the main text. (§II.E, p. 10; §III.C, pp. 53–55.)
- **APS rule.** Display numbered, important, long or complicated equations. Punctuate displayed mathematics as part of the sentence. APS permits consecutive Arabic numbering or numbering by main section. Consecutive numbers such as (1) and (2) work well for a short report. (§II.G.4, p. 17; §III.A.1, p. 47; §IV.C, pp. 61–62.)
- **APS rule.** Set single-letter variables in italics and standard function names such as `exp` and `ln` upright. Set multiletter descriptive subscripts upright. APS allows either face for most single-letter descriptive subscripts and asks for consistency. Use parentheses where a slashed fraction could be ambiguous. (§IV.A.1.b, pp. 59–60; §IV.B, p. 61; §IV.E.2, pp. 65–66.)
- **APS recommendation and rule.** Prefer SI where physical units are needed. Set ordinary unit symbols upright, with a space after the number and thin spaces between compound unit factors. Use negative powers or parentheses to clarify compound denominators. (§III.D, pp. 55–56.)

## Figures, captions and tables

- **APS rule.** Cite figures and tables in order of first mention. Use Arabic numerals for figures and Roman numerals for tables. Use “Figure” at the start of a sentence, “Fig.” elsewhere, and “Table” in either position. Label figure panels (a), (b), etc. and explain each panel in the caption. (§II.G.3, p. 17; §II.U.2, p. 44.)
- **APS rule.** Give each figure a caption intelligible independently of the main text. Explain symbols and curves in the legend or caption. Label axes with consistent notation and put applicable units in parentheses. Include a leading zero for decimal values below one and keep decimal precision consistent across ticks on an axis. (§II.U.2–3, p. 44.)
- **APS recommendation.** Size figures for the printed column and use line styles or markers as well as colour to distinguish series. Prefer accessible palettes and check legibility at final size. The guide gives 2 mm for lettering height and 1 mm for data-point diameter as recommended minima. (§II.U.1, p. 43; §II.U.4, p. 44.)
- **APS rule and recommendation.** Use a self-contained caption for each numbered table. Explain table-wide notation there, with definitions already given in the body optional. Put units in parentheses in the headings and keep rules sparse. (§II.T, p. 36; §II.T.2–4, p. 37.)

## References

- **APS rule.** Use bracketed Arabic citation numbers in order of first appearance and list sources at the end. For journal articles, use the standard journal abbreviation, bold volume, first page or article number, and year in parentheses. (§II.G.2.a, p. 15; §II.S.1.a, p. 27; §II.S.4, pp. 28–29.)
- **APS recommendation.** Include article titles where practical. APS strongly encourages them and specifies sentence case, upright type and no quotation marks when included. Use published peer-reviewed articles in preference to their e-print versions. (§II.S.1–2, p. 27; §II.S.8, p. 31.)

## Coursework application

The [Warwick marking sheet](warwick-marking-sheet.pdf) covers presentation, background, methodology, analysis and results, and discussion and conclusions. Make figures readable at final size, explain uncertainty and compare results quantitatively with expectations where the evidence permits. Follow the assignment’s format and length rules, including how references are counted. The [bundled REVTeX template](../paper/paper.tex) uses standalone section headings. Follow the active brief if it prescribes a different format.

Verify publication requirements against the current [PRL author instructions](https://journals.aps.org/prl/authors) for an actual submission.
