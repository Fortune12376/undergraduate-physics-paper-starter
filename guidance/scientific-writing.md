# Scientific writing and review

Use the active brief, rubric, and template for format, length, citation style, permitted assistance, and disclosure. Apply the guidance below where it supports the scientific question. The default order is Abstract, Introduction (including theory), Methodology, Results and Discussion, Conclusion, References, and appendices where useful and permitted. Choose subsections only when they help the reader follow the investigation.

- **Abstract.** Write it last. Give the motivation, objective, approach, principal quantitative findings with relevant uncertainties, and physical conclusion.
- **Introduction.** Narrow from the scientific context and literature to the research question. Define quantities as they appear. Present the governing model, assumptions, predictions, scaling or limits that connect theory to observables. Include derivations when they help establish that connection. Preview the approach if helpful.
- **Methodology.** Give enough detail to reproduce the work and justify consequential choices. For experiments, specify apparatus, conditions, acquisition, controlled variables, precautions and calibrations actually used. Identify what each check demonstrates. For computation, describe the model, algorithm, implementation details affecting results, initial and boundary conditions, numerical parameters and sampling. Explain processing, selection rules, fits and parameter estimation. State how uncertainties were estimated and propagated, including consequential correlations. Give the calculation or procedure that was used. Where relevant, describe checks against exact solutions, benchmarks, conservation laws or limiting cases, and explain how convergence, discretisation, sampling and parameter sensitivity were assessed. Record missing evidence and pending checks explicitly.
- **Results and Discussion.** Order findings around the scientific questions. Give values with units, justified precision and relevant uncertainties. Use legible figures and tables with clear labels, conditions, and definitions of symbols, curves and error bars. Refer to each in the text and use prose to explain the finding. Place physical interpretation and quantitative comparisons with theory or published results near the evidence. Assess discrepancies against uncertainty and quantify dominant effects where possible. Distinguish statistical scatter, systematic measurement error, numerical error and model limitations. Report the outcomes of relevant validation, convergence and sensitivity checks. Identify which explanations have evidence, which remain hypotheses, and how remaining limitations affect the conclusions. Suggest specific improvements where scientifically justified.
- **Conclusion.** Answer the original question with the principal quantitative findings, relevant uncertainty, the strength of support for predictions, and physical significance. Preserve material qualifications and use evidence already presented.
- **References and appendices.** Verify that sources support the claims, models, equations, methods and comparisons they accompany. Provide traceable bibliographic metadata. Keep essential reasoning and methodological limitations in the main text. Use permitted appendices for supporting derivations, technical details, code or additional results when useful.

During the existing review, trace objectives through methods, evidence and conclusions. Prioritise missing scientific information, irreproducible procedures, unjustified choices, unexplained uncertainties, missing applicable numerical checks, unsupported interpretations, and weak comparisons or limitation assessments. Check that claimed controls and analyses were performed and that conclusions reflect their actual scope. Flag low-value subdivisions or repetition only where they impair clarity. Continuous prose is acceptable whenever it covers the relevant science.
